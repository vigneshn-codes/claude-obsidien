# Concept — Scarcity & Urgency

**Source book:** $100M Offers, Section IV
**Type:** Offer enhancer
**Related concepts:** Grand Slam Offer, Bonuses, Reason Why

## Definition
Two distinct psychological levers that compress the buyer's decision window:
- **Scarcity** = limit of *supply* (how many can have it).
- **Urgency** = limit of *time* (when they can have it).

## Why They Work
Without them, "I'll think about it" wins. Loss aversion is roughly 2× as strong as gain seeking — making the cost of *not deciding* concrete and imminent overcomes inertia.

## Scarcity Patterns
- Limited **seats** ("only 10 spots this cohort").
- Limited **inventory** ("only 200 units").
- Limited **bonuses** ("first 50 buyers get…").
- Limited **access tiers** ("only 3 advisory slots").

## Urgency Patterns
- **Rolling cohorts** — enrollment closes Friday.
- **Pricing deadlines** — price increases on the 1st.
- **Exploding bonuses** — bonus expires at midnight.
- **Seasonal urgency** — "New Year intake closes Jan 15."
- **Personalized urgency** — "your custom plan expires in 72 hours."

## The "Reason Why" Rule
Every scarcity or urgency claim **must come with a believable reason.**
- Bad: "Only 10 spots available!" (sounds invented)
- Good: "I personally coach this cohort and I can only handle 10 clients/month." (anchored to a real constraint)

Without a reason why, scarcity reads as manipulation and *decreases* trust.

## Combining Scarcity and Urgency
Stacked, they compound:
> *"Only 25 seats available, doors close Friday at midnight, $1,000 bonus expires Wednesday."*

But: never stack to the point of disbelief. One credible constraint beats three implausible ones.

## Long-Term Caveat
Faked scarcity erodes brand. Hormozi's recommendation: build your business so scarcity is **structurally real** (limited delivery capacity, limited cohort design, limited advisory time).
