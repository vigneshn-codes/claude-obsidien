# Concept — Guarantee Taxonomy

**Source book:** $100M Offers, Section IV
**Type:** Risk-reversal framework
**Related concepts:** Grand Slam Offer, Bonuses, Perceived Likelihood

## Definition
A **guarantee** is any explicit risk-reversal mechanism that shifts perceived risk from the buyer to the seller. Hormozi argues guarantees are the highest-ROI single enhancer in offer design.

## The Four Types

### 1. Unconditional Guarantees
- "Full money back, no questions, any reason."
- Pros: maximum risk reversal, highest trust.
- Cons: highest refund exposure; attracts tire-kickers.

### 2. Conditional Guarantees
- "Money back **if** you do X and don't get Y."
- Tied to demonstrable buyer effort and a measurable outcome.
- Pros: filters non-committed buyers; protects margin.
- Cons: requires clear, trackable conditions.
- Premium variants:
  - **Double-your-money-back** (if conditions met).
  - **Refund + bonus** (you keep the bonus product even after refund).

### 3. Anti-Guarantees
- "No refunds — here's why."
- Used when ethical/operational constraints require it (e.g., consumed information products, custom builds).
- Must explain the reason transparently.
- Counterintuitively *raises* trust when paired with strong proof.

### 4. Implied / Performance Guarantees
- Pay-on-results: "We get paid only when you hit X."
- Revenue share, equity stake, performance fees.
- Best for high-trust, high-LTV B2B engagements.

## Stacking Guarantees
Multiple guarantee types compound:
- *Unconditional 30-day refund* + *conditional "double-your-money-back" at day 90* + *anti-guarantee on consumed bonuses*.

Each layer covers a different objection.

## The Effort Multiplier
The longer the guarantee window, the higher the conversion uplift **and** the lower the refund rate per buyer — because long windows attract committed buyers who actually use the product.

## Common Failure Modes
- Vague conditions ("if you really try").
- Hidden refund process buried in fine print (trust destroyer).
- Guarantee stronger than the operation can support (cash flow blow-up).

## Hormozi's Rule of Thumb
> "If you're not willing to guarantee an outcome, you don't believe in it. Fix the offer until you can."
