# Concept — Starving Crowd

**Source book:** $100M Offers, Section II (Pricing)
**Type:** Market-selection principle
**Related concepts:** Four Indicators of a Great Market, Grand Slam Offer

## Definition
A **starving crowd** is a market with such intense demand that even a mediocre offer can succeed. Hormozi adapts the principle from Gary Halbert's classic question: *"If you could only have one advantage opening a hamburger stand, what would it be?"* Answer: not the meat, the bun, or the location — but **a starving crowd.**

## The Hierarchy
Hormozi orders the levers of business success from most to least important:

```
Market   >   Offer   >   Brand   >   Copy
```

A great offer in a bad market still fails. A bad offer in a great market often still wins.

## The Four Indicators of a Great Market
1. **Massive Pain** — the prospect has a desperate need, not just a want.
2. **Purchasing Power** — they have money, or easy access to money.
3. **Easy to Target** — they congregate in identifiable channels (subreddits, conferences, LinkedIn groups, search terms).
4. **Growing** — tailwinds, not headwinds. New entrants joining the market faster than they leave.

If a market fails 2+ of these, walk away.

## Sub-Niche, Don't Compete Niche
Once a starving crowd is found, **commit to one sub-niche** and dominate it before expanding. Hormozi calls this earning a "virtual monopoly" in a narrow segment.

Examples:
- Not "fitness" → "post-pregnancy fitness for 6-figure female founders"
- Not "marketing agency" → "Facebook ads for boutique med spas"
- Not "coaching" → "executive coaching for newly promoted VPs at series-B SaaS"

## Why Niching Down Works
- Easier targeting (Indicator 3 satisfied).
- Easier testimonials (every case study sounds like your prospect).
- Easier copy ("for *you* specifically" beats "for everyone").
- Premium pricing (specialist > generalist).
- Faster word of mouth (small ponds gossip).

## The Common Trap
Founders confuse *interest* with *demand*. A market with lots of free content consumers is not a starving crowd unless they're already paying *someone* for the result. Find the spend, not the scroll.
