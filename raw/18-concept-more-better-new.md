# Concept — More, Better, New

**Source book:** $100M Leads
**Type:** Decision heuristic
**Related concepts:** Core Four, Rule of 100, Lead Getters

## Definition
When stuck on growth (leads, revenue, output), there are only three knobs:
1. **More** — do more of what you're already doing.
2. **Better** — do what you're already doing, better.
3. **New** — add a new strategy/channel.

That's it. Every "what should I do next?" decision routes through these three.

## The Default Order
1. **More** first. Most under-performance comes from under-volume, not bad strategy.
2. **Better** second. Optimize creative, copy, conversion, fulfillment.
3. **New** last. Adding channels before maxing the current ones spreads attention too thin.

## Why This Order
Adding a *new* channel is the most expensive option in attention, infrastructure, and risk. It also feels like progress when really it's procrastination from doing more of the boring work that already works.

## Worked Diagnostic
> "My cold email is bringing in 3 leads/week."
- **More:** are you sending 100/day? If not — send more. Stop here.
- **Better:** if yes, are your subject lines working? Iterate the opener. Stop here.
- **New:** only if both above are maxed, add cold DMs.

## The Test for "Maxed"
A channel is "maxed" when:
- You're hitting daily volume targets consistently.
- Conversion rates have plateaued for 4+ weeks despite iteration.
- The next marginal hour spent on it yields lower ROI than starting a new channel.

## Application to Money
The same three knobs apply to revenue:
- **More** customers (lead gen).
- **Better** conversion / LTV (offer + delivery).
- **New** revenue stream (upsell, downsell, cross-sell).

## The Counter-Trap
Constantly adding *New* is a form of FOMO disguised as ambition. Discipline = staying on *More* and *Better* longer than feels comfortable.
