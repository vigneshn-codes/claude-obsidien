# Concept — The Four Constraints

**Source book:** $100M Money Models
**Type:** Diagnostic framework
**Related concepts:** CAC Payback, Theory of Constraints, Lead Getters

## Definition
Every business is bottlenecked by **one of four** constraints at any given time. Identifying the binding constraint determines what to work on next. Working on a non-binding constraint produces no growth.

## The Four
1. **Leads** — not enough qualified prospects entering the system.
2. **Sales** — leads enter but don't convert.
3. **Delivery** — customers convert but don't stay (low LTV / high churn).
4. **Profit** — customers stay but the unit economics don't compound.

## The Diagnostic Sequence
Ask in order:
1. Do we have enough leads? (If no → Leads constraint.)
2. Are leads converting? (If no → Sales constraint.)
3. Are customers staying and expanding? (If no → Delivery constraint.)
4. Is each dollar of revenue producing a profit dollar that compounds? (If no → Profit constraint.)

The first "no" is the binding constraint. Everything else is downstream.

## Why This Order Matters
A business with a delivery constraint cannot be fixed by more ads. A business with a profit constraint cannot be fixed by more leads. Mismatching effort to constraint is the most common reason businesses stall at 7-figures.

## Mapping Constraints to Systems
Hormozi pairs each constraint with a *system* in $100M Money Models:

| Constraint | System |
|---|---|
| Leads | The Leads System (Chapter 8) |
| Sales | The Sales System |
| Delivery | The Delivery System (Chapter 10) |
| Profit | The Profit System / Money Model |

## Constraint Migration
Solving one constraint usually exposes the next. A business that fixes leads will quickly become sales-constrained. This is **healthy** — the business is growing into a new bottleneck. The work is recognizing the migration and re-diagnosing.

## The Anti-Pattern
Building elaborate systems for the constraint you *enjoy* working on instead of the one that's actually binding. Founders with marketing backgrounds over-invest in leads; founders with ops backgrounds over-invest in delivery. The discipline is doing the *uncomfortable* work the diagnostic reveals.
