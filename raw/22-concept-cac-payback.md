# Concept — CAC Payback < 30 Days

**Source book:** $100M Money Models
**Type:** Unit economics principle
**Related concepts:** Four Constraints, LTV, Money Model

## Definition
**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) payback** is the time between spending to acquire a customer and earning back that spend in revenue (or, more strictly, gross profit). Hormozi's standard:

> "If you make more from a customer in the first 30 days than it cost to acquire them, you can outspend every competitor — forever."

## Why 30 Days?
- Aligns with monthly cash cycles for most businesses.
- Short enough to recycle ad spend within the same month.
- Long enough to capture initial upsells/back-end revenue.

## The Compounding Effect
If CAC payback is sub-30 days, you can:
1. Spend $X on ads in week 1.
2. Earn $X+ back by week 4.
3. Reinvest in week 5.
4. Repeat indefinitely with no external capital.

The business funds its own growth. Competitors who pay back CAC in 6 months can't keep up on bid prices, and lose share.

## Levers to Reduce CAC Payback
1. **Front-end pricing** — charge enough on the initial purchase that CAC is recouped immediately.
2. **Order bumps** — small add-ons at checkout.
3. **Upsells** — higher-tier offers presented post-purchase.
4. **Downsells** — fallback offers when premium upsells fail.
5. **Annual / Paid-in-full plans** — bring forward 12+ months of revenue with a discount incentive.
6. **Deposits / prepayments** — collect cash before delivery.

## Front-End vs Back-End Money Model
- **Front-end:** cheap or free entry-point optimized for *velocity* (low CAC, fast yes).
- **Back-end:** higher-priced offers optimized for *LTV* (upgrades, expansions, advisory).

The front-end's job is to recover CAC. The back-end's job is to fund profit + reinvestment.

## The Formula in Practice
```
30-Day Revenue per Customer  -  CAC  >  0   →   You can outspend competitors
30-Day Revenue per Customer  -  CAC  <  0   →   You need external capital to grow
```

## Common Failure Modes
- Measuring revenue (not gross profit) — masks fulfillment costs.
- Including unblended LTV — hides the cash flow problem.
- Optimizing for CAC reduction at the expense of customer quality (bad LTV, high churn, lower payback over time).

## Hormozi's Practical Test
> "If you can't afford to lose money on the first sale and *still* be profitable by day 30, your money model is broken."
