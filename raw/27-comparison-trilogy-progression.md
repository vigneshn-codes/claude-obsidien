# Comparison — How the $100M Trilogy Builds on Itself

The three $100M books form an intentionally sequenced curriculum. Each answers a distinct question and depends on the prior. Reading them out of order works, but reading them in order shows the architecture.

## The Three-Book Spine

| # | Book | Year | Core Question | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $100M Offers | 2021 | *What do I sell, and how do I package it so it sells itself?* | An irresistible **offer** |
| 2 | $100M Leads | 2023 | *How do I get strangers to discover my offer?* | A scalable **lead system** |
| 3 | $100M Money Models | 2025 | *How do I get paid enough, fast enough, to outspend competitors?* | A compounding **money model** |

## How They Compose
- **Offer × Leads = Revenue.** A great offer with no leads is a private secret. Many leads with a bad offer is a leaky funnel.
- **(Offer × Leads) × Money Model = Compounding Business.** A working funnel that loses money on the first sale can't scale without external capital. A money model that pays back CAC fast lets the engine run on its own profits.

## Concept Threads That Run Through All Three

### Thread 1 — Perceived Value
- Offers: introduces the Value Equation.
- Leads: lead magnets are valuable because they obey the Value Equation.
- Money Models: pricing tiers are designed to maximize perceived value per dollar.

### Thread 2 — Volume
- Offers: divergent thinking (volume of ideas → trim).
- Leads: Rule of 100 (volume of actions → leads).
- Money Models: volume of customers + reinvestment loop → growth.

### Thread 3 — Risk Reversal
- Offers: guarantees move risk to the seller.
- Leads: lead magnets give value before asking for anything.
- Money Models: low-friction front-end offers reduce buyer risk on entry.

## Pacing Recommendation
- **Pre-revenue founders:** read Offers first, then Leads. Money Models is the third pass.
- **Established founders (>$1M):** read Money Models first to diagnose the binding constraint, then back-fill Offers/Leads where needed.
- **Marketers / operators (not founders):** read Leads first, then Offers, then Money Models as a strategic capstone.

## What Each Book Is *Not* About
- Offers is not a marketing channel book.
- Leads is not an offer-design book.
- Money Models is not a personal finance book — it's a *business* unit economics book.

Confusing scope is the most common reason readers report disappointment with any single book in the trilogy.
