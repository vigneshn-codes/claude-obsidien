# Bonuses & The Value Stack

**Summary:** Additional solutions stacked on top of the core offer to multiply perceived value without proportionally raising delivery cost — always beats discounting.

**Category:** Offer Design
**Sources:** `13-concept-bonuses-stack.md`, `01-100m-offers.md`
**Related:** [[grand-slam-offer]], [[trim-and-stack]], [[guarantee-taxonomy]], [[scarcity-urgency]], [[magic-naming]]

## Why Bonuses Beat Discounts

| Discount | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Reduces price — anchor drops forever | Keeps price intact — anchor stays high |
| Attracts price shoppers (low LTV) | Attracts value shoppers (high LTV) |
| "This thing isn't worth the original price" | "This thing comes with even more value" |
| Hard to un-do once set | Stack or remove per campaign |

> "Never discount. Instead, add more value until the price feels like a steal."

---

## How to Design a Great Bonus

Each bonus must pass five tests:

1. **Solves a specific objection** — map every bonus to a stated or unstated resistance point.
2. **Has its own distinct name** — "The 90-Day Plateau Protocol," not "Bonus #3."
3. **Has its own ascribed dollar value** — present it as a standalone product the customer would pay for.
4. **Is a tool, template, or shortcut** — low marginal cost to deliver, high perceived value to receive.
5. **Stacks naturally** — a bonus that requires a completely different skill to use is friction, not value.

---

## The "Stack Slide" Format

Use this structure when presenting your full offer:

```
Core Offer:     [Name + outcome + price anchor]         $X,000 value
Bonus 1:        [Name] — [one-sentence outcome]         $XXX value
Bonus 2:        [Name] — [one-sentence outcome]         $XXX value
Bonus 3:        [Name] — [one-sentence outcome]         $XXX value
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL VALUE:                                          $X,XXX
YOUR PRICE:                                             $XXX
```

The visual gap between Total Value and Your Price creates the anchor effect. The ratio matters — aim for at least 5:1 perceived value to price.

## Bonus Source Library for Fitness / Workout Products

| Bonus Idea | Objection It Kills |
|---|---|
| Done-for-you meal prep template | "I don't know what to eat" |
| Plateau Buster Protocol | "What if I stop seeing results?" |
| 30-day gym anxiety guide | "I'll feel embarrassed as a beginner" |
| Supplement cheat sheet | "I don't know what's worth buying" |
| Travel workout library | "I travel too much to stick with a program" |
| Home workout substitute list | "I can't always make it to the gym" |
| Sleep optimization guide | "I know recovery matters but don't know how to improve it" |
| Community access | "I'll lose motivation without accountability" |

---

## The Limit Rule

Too many bonuses dilutes the stack. Each bonus that *doesn't* kill a specific objection is noise. Rule of thumb: **enough bonuses to neutralize every major objection, no more.**

If you can't answer "what specific objection does this bonus destroy?" — cut it.

---

## Hormozi's Heuristic

> "If the buyer can immediately answer 'what does this fix for me?' for every bonus on the slide, you've stacked correctly."
