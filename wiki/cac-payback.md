# CAC Payback (< 30 Days)

**Summary:** The north-star unit economics metric — if you recover your customer acquisition cost within 30 days, you can outspend every competitor indefinitely.

**Category:** Money Models
**Sources:** `22-concept-cac-payback.md`, `03-100m-money-models.md`, `05-key-frameworks.md`
**Related:** [[four-constraints]], [[money-model]], [[grand-slam-offer]], [[value-equation]]

---

## The Core Principle

> "If you make more from a customer in the first 30 days than it cost to acquire them, you can outspend every competitor — forever."

**CAC** = Customer Acquisition Cost (total marketing + sales spend to acquire one customer).
**Payback period** = days between spending CAC and earning it back in gross profit.

Hormozi's target: **< 30 days**.

---

## Why 30 Days?

- Aligns with monthly cash cycles
- Short enough to recycle ad spend within the same billing period
- Long enough to capture initial upsells and back-end revenue

---

## The Compounding Logic

With sub-30-day payback:

```
Week 1:  Spend $1,000 on ads
Week 2:  Earn $1,200 back from new customers
Week 5:  Reinvest $1,200 → earn $1,440
Week 9:  Reinvest $1,440 → earn $1,728
…
```

The business funds its own growth. A competitor with 6-month CAC payback cannot keep up — they run out of cash or need external capital to scale.

---

## The Decision Rule

```
30-Day Gross Profit per Customer  −  CAC  >  0   →   Outspend competitors
30-Day Gross Profit per Customer  −  CAC  <  0   →   Need external capital to grow
```

*Note: use **gross profit**, not revenue. Revenue masks fulfillment costs.*

---

## Levers to Reduce CAC Payback Period

| Lever | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Front-end pricing** | Charge enough upfront to recover CAC immediately |
| **Order bumps** | Small add-ons at checkout (e.g., "Add nutrition tracking for $9/mo") |
| **Upsells** | Higher-tier offer presented immediately post-purchase |
| **Downsells** | Lower-priced fallback when premium upsell is declined |
| **Annual / paid-in-full plans** | Collect 12 months of revenue upfront with a discount incentive |
| **Deposits** | Collect cash before delivery begins |

---

## Front-End / Back-End Architecture

| Stage | Purpose | Design Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Front-end** | Entry-point offer | Low CAC, fast conversion, recovers CAC |
| **Back-end** | Higher-priced follow-on offers | Generates LTV, funds profit + reinvestment |

The front-end's *only* job is to recover CAC. Profitability comes from the back-end.

---

## Common Failure Modes

| Mistake | Why It Breaks |
|---|---|
| Measuring revenue instead of gross profit | Hides fulfillment costs; looks profitable when it isn't |
| Using blended LTV to justify bad 30-day economics | Cash flow is a now problem; LTV is a later assumption |
| Optimizing for lowest CAC at cost of customer quality | Cheap acquisition often produces high churn → lower payback |
| No back-end offers | All profit must come from front-end → forces high price → kills conversion |

---

## Application to Workout / Fitness Software

| Scenario | CAC Payback Math | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| $50 Meta ad spend to acquire 1 user at $9.99/mo | 5-month payback → broken | Add annual plan at $79 (pays back in month 1) |
| $100 affiliate commission, user pays $149 for program | Day-1 payback → healthy | Scale affiliate spend aggressively |
| $200 CAC from content + team cost, user stays avg 3 months at $29/mo | 2.3-month payback → borderline | Add $47 order bump at checkout OR raise price |

---

## Hormozi's Test

> "If you can't afford to lose money on the first sale and still be profitable by day 30, your money model is broken."
