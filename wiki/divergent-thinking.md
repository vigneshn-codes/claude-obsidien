# Divergent vs. Convergent Thinking

**Summary:** The cognitive mode distinction Hormozi applies to offer design — divergent thinking generates many possible solutions, convergent thinking selects the best one. Offer creation is divergent; treating it as convergent is the most common mistake.

**Category:** Mindset & Identity
**Sources:** `17-concept-divergent-thinking.md`, `01-100m-offers.md`
**Related:** [[trim-and-stack]], [[grand-slam-offer]], [[magic-naming]]

---

## The Distinction

| Mode | Characteristics | Right Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Convergent** | One right answer. Math, code, accounting, logistics. Judgment-first. | Execution, optimization, selection |
| **Divergent** | Many right answers. Marketing, naming, offer design. Quantity-first. | Brainstorming, creation, ideation |

**Offer design is divergent.** Treating it as convergent — hunting for the single "correct" offer — causes founders to freeze.

---

## The Workflow

1. **Diverge (25 min)** — generate 20+ candidates. No judgment. Quantity only.
2. **Don't score yet** — premature filtering kills the best ideas. They're often the weirdest ones.
3. **Score (25 min)** — apply the [[value-equation]] lens to each candidate.
4. **Converge** — pick the top combination. Cut the rest.
5. **Ship** — publish, run reps, iterate.

**Never mix modes in the same sitting.** Judging while generating is the creativity killer.

| Ad hooks | 30+ headline variations | A/B test top 3 |

| Bonuses | 20+ possible bonuses | Stack only the objection-killers |

---

## The Cognitive Switch

Hormozi's recommendation:
- **Time-box each mode.** 25 minutes divergent (no judgment). 25 minutes convergent (ruthless filtering).
- The switch is the point — it's artificial but effective. Your brain cannot do both simultaneously.

---

## The Anti-Pattern

> "I'll write the perfect offer now."

The perfect offer doesn't exist as a single inspired insight. It's the **survivor of 50 weaker siblings.** First-draft thinking produces average-feeling offers because the average person's first instinct is the average idea.

---

## Application to Software Development (Vignesh's Context)

Divergent thinking applies directly to feature design and technical problem-solving:

- **Feature brainstorm:** Generate 15 possible ways to solve a user problem. Converge on the one with highest [[value-equation]] score.
- **Naming your app/product:** 50 name ideas → MAGIC filter → gut check → test.
- **Pricing model design:** 10 possible pricing structures → model CAC payback on each → pick.
- **Onboarding flow:** 20 possible first-session experiences → test for time-to-first-win.
