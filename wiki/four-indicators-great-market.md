# Four Indicators of a Great Market

**Summary:** A pre-offer filter that determines whether a market is worth entering before any product, offer, or brand is built.

**Category:** Market Strategy
**Sources:** `05-key-frameworks.md`, `01-100m-offers.md`, `16-concept-starving-crowd.md`
**Related:** [[starving-crowd]], [[grand-slam-offer]], [[magic-naming]]

---

## The Four Indicators

A market must clear all four before building an offer. Two or more failures → walk away.

| # | Indicator | What to Look For |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Massive Pain** | Desperate need, not a casual want. Active spending already happening. |
| 2 | **Purchasing Power** | Target audience has money or easy access to it (credit, employer budget, etc.) |
| 3 | **Easy to Target** | They congregate in identifiable channels: specific subreddits, LinkedIn groups, conferences, Facebook groups, YouTube search terms |
| 4 | **Growing** | Market is expanding — new entrants, rising search trends, cultural tailwinds |

---

## Indicator Deep Dives

### 1. Massive Pain
**How to verify:**
- Are people already paying someone for a solution to this problem?
- Is the problem urgent ("I need to fix this now") or merely interesting ("I'd like to fix this someday")?
- Are there active communities complaining about this problem?

**Fitness example:** "I want to lose 10 lbs" is a want. "I'm embarrassed to take my shirt off at my own wedding in 8 weeks" is pain.

### 2. Purchasing Power
**How to verify:**
- What are others in this market charging, and are customers paying?
- Is the demographic salaried, professional, or otherwise funded?
- Is the pain expensive enough that buying a solution is obvious?

**Red flag:** Markets full of free content consumers who never pay. Enthusiasts ≠ buyers.

### 3. Easy to Target
**How to verify:**
- Can you find them on one or two platforms with precise filters?
- Are there trade publications, conferences, or associations they belong to?
- Can you describe your ideal customer in one sentence and find 10,000 of them tomorrow?

### 4. Growing
**How to verify:**
- Google Trends — is search volume rising?
- Are new competitors entering the market? (Good sign)
- Are there cultural or demographic tailwinds? (e.g., aging population → senior fitness; remote work → home gym)

---

## The Hierarchy Reminder

```
Market  >  Offer  >  Brand  >  Copy
```

Optimizing offer, brand, and copy in a bad market is wasted effort. Pick the market first.

---

## Application to Fitness Software

| Niche to Evaluate | Massive Pain? | Purchasing Power? | Easy to Target? | Growing? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Powerlifters (meet prep automation) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (r/powerlifting, meet orgs) | ~ | Viable |
| General "get fit" app | ~ | ✓ | ✗ (too broad) | ~ | Avoid |
| Runners returning post-injury | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (running clubs, physio referrals) | ✓ | Strong |
| Corporate wellness (desk workers) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (LinkedIn, HR channels) | ✓ | Strong |
| Gym owners (B2B software) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (IHRSA, local gyms) | ✓ | Very Strong |
