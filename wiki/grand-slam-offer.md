# Grand Slam Offer

**Summary:** A unique, premium-priced, risk-reversed bundle of value so compelling that price comparison becomes impossible — the offer exists in its own category.

**Category:** Offer Design
**Sources:** `08-concept-grand-slam-offer.md`, `01-100m-offers.md`, `05-key-frameworks.md`
**Related:** [[value-equation]], [[trim-and-stack]], [[bonuses-stack]], [[guarantee-taxonomy]], [[scarcity-urgency]], [[magic-naming]]

---

## Core Idea

> "Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no."

Most businesses compete on price because their offers are interchangeable. A Grand Slam Offer breaks the comparison. When someone tries to shop around, there's nothing to compare it to.

**The test:** If your offer survives a Google search at the same price, it isn't a Grand Slam Offer yet.

---

## The Five Parts

| Part | What It Does |
|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | Names the transformation the customer wants (not the deliverable you provide) |
| **Increased Likelihood of Achievement** | Proof, guarantees, named mechanisms — makes success feel inevitable |
| **Decreased Time Delay** | Speed to first value; quick wins baked into delivery |
| **Decreased Effort & Sacrifice** | Done-for-you, templates, removed prerequisites |
| **Enhancers** | Bonuses + Guarantees + Scarcity + Urgency + MAGIC Name |

The first four are the [[value-equation]] in offer form. The Enhancers are the multipliers.

---

## Construction Process (Step by Step)

1. **Pick one niche.** (See [[starving-crowd]] — market selection first.)
2. **Name the Dream Outcome.** State it in the customer's language, not yours.
3. **List every problem** the prospect faces on the path to that outcome.
   - Logistical ("I don't know what to eat")
   - Mental/emotional ("I'll lose motivation by week 3")
   - Tactical ("I don't know how to program progressive overload")
   - Social ("I'll look stupid at the gym")
4. **Convert each problem into a solution.** Every problem is a potential deliverable.
5. **List delivery vehicles** for each solution (1-on-1 call, video, template, app, group, community, etc.).
6. **[[trim-and-stack]]** — cut low-value/high-cost vehicles. Stack high-perceived-value/low-cost vehicles.
7. **Layer the Enhancers:**
   - Stack [[bonuses-stack]]
   - Add [[guarantee-taxonomy]]
   - Manufacture [[scarcity-urgency]]
   - Name it with [[magic-naming]]

## Why Premium Pricing Helps

Counter-intuitively, higher prices produce:
- Better clients (more committed, fewer complaints)
- Better margins (room to deliver exceptional results)
- Better positioning (price signals quality)
- More referrals (people brag about premium purchases, not cheap ones)

> "The cheapest customers complain the most. The most expensive customers complain the least."

## Worked Example — Workout Programming App (B2C)

**Avatar:** Male, 28–38, works desk job, wants to add muscle without living in the gym.

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | "Add 20 lbs of lean muscle in 16 weeks without quitting your job or your social life" |
| Likelihood | 1,200 user before/afters, 4.9★ App Store rating, money-back guarantee |
| Speed | Day-1 "baseline workout" — feel the protocol working immediately |
| Effort | 4 workouts/week, 45 min each, auto-programmed, no spreadsheet required |
| Bonus 1 | Nutrition targets calculator ($97 value) — "so you don't have to track macros manually" |
| Bonus 2 | "Plateau Buster Protocol" ($67 value) — for weeks when progress stalls |
| Guarantee | "Hit your first PR in 30 days or full refund" |
| Scarcity | "Founding member cohort — 200 spots, closes Jan 31" |
| Name | *The 16-Week Desk Jockey Muscle Accelerator* |

## Counter-Examples (What Breaks a Grand Slam Offer)

- Any single piece can be bought cheaper elsewhere ❌
- The guarantee is vague ("we'll work together until you're happy") ❌
- No named mechanism — sounds identical to every other program ❌
- Priced identically to competitors — destroys the "own category" positioning ❌
