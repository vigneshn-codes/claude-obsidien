## Standards Heuristic

> "We don't rise to the standards we have when others are watching; we fall to the standards we have when no one is."

**Application:** Audit the work nobody sees. The private standard is the leading indicator of public outcomes. What is the quality of your:
- Code written at 11pm on a Friday?
- Workout when no coach is present?
- Content draft when there's no deadline?

---

## The Discipline / Confidence Loop

> "You don't become confident by shouting affirmations. You become confident by having undeniable proof that you are who you say you are."

Confidence is not a precondition for action — it's the *output* of action. The loop:
```
Commit → Act → Produce evidence → Build self-trust → Commit at higher level
```

Affirmations short-circuit the loop; they produce the feeling without the evidence.

## The Winner's Paradox

> "The winner's mindset sits in the uncomfortable place between two surface-level contradictions: extreme paranoia in the present and unshakeable faith in the future."

- **Paranoia in the present:** intense scrutiny of current results, processes, and standards. No complacency.
- **Faith in the future:** certainty that the compounding of disciplined daily action will eventually produce the outcome.

These feel contradictory but are mutually reinforcing. Paranoia without faith produces anxiety. Faith without paranoia produces complacency.

## Boredom as Signal

> "Boredom is the price of mastery."

The feeling of boredom doing a proven activity is the signal you're doing it right. The temptation to add *New* channels, features, or strategies is often resistance to the boring repetition that actually works. See [[more-better-new]].

---

## Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

> "The cost of not deciding is greater than the cost of deciding wrong."

Indecision is a decision — to let the situation decay, miss the window, or defer to someone else. A wrong decision that generates data is worth more than infinite analysis that generates none.

## Focus

> "You can have anything you want — you just can't have everything you want."
> "Focus is saying no to one hundred good ideas to say yes to the one great one."

**Application:** In early-stage businesses, every resource (time, attention, capital) spent on a non-binding constraint is stolen from the binding constraint. See [[four-constraints]].

---

## Persistence as Strategy

> "I cannot lose if I do not quit."

This isn't bravado — it's a logical claim. In a game where improvement compounds over time, the only way to fail is to stop playing. Hormozi treats persistence as the baseline, not the differentiator.

---

## Work Ethic

> "Set a timer for 20 hours. Actually try for 20 hours before you quit. It's not hard; you haven't even tried yet."

The "20 hours" heuristic is a call to distinguish "this didn't work" from "I tried this once and it was uncomfortable." Most skill acquisition, channel development, and offer testing requires 20× the effort that most people invest before declaring failure.

---

## Leverage Hierarchy

> "The four leverages: labor, capital, code, content. Stack them in that order."

For software builders, this is particularly relevant:
- **Labor** — other people doing work
- **Capital** — money working for you
- **Code** — software that scales without marginal cost
- **Content** — attention that compounds over time

Code and content are the highest-leverage activities for a solo technical founder.
