> "If you make more money from a customer in the first 30 days than it cost to acquire them, you can outspend every competitor — forever."

A money model is the answer to: *How do I get paid enough, fast enough, to scale?* Where $100M Offers built the offer and $100M Leads filled the pipeline, the money model tunes the **economics** that let both compound.

## Key Money Model Concepts

### Front-End / Back-End Stacking

**Front-End:**
- Cheap or free entry-point optimized for velocity
- Low price → low CAC → fast "yes"
- Goal: recover CAC before Day 30

**Back-End:**
- Higher-priced offers presented after initial purchase
- Upsells, expansions, advisory tiers, annual plans
- Goal: generate LTV, fund profit + reinvestment

### CAC Payback < 30 Days
The north-star metric. See [[cac-payback]].

### Upfront Cash Maximization
Mechanisms to bring future revenue forward:
- **Pay-in-full bonuses** — 20–40% discount for annual commitment
- **Annual plans** — collect 12 months upfront
- **Deposits** — collect cash before delivery begins
- **Order bumps** — small add-ons at checkout
- **Prepayment incentives** — "Pay in full, get X free"

### LTV Lever Mechanics
- **Price increases** — annual or biannual pricing nudge for existing customers
- **Retention design** — onboarding rituals, quick wins, community (see [[six-week-challenge]] for gym model)
- **Expansion offers** — upsell existing customers to higher tiers
- **Reactivation campaigns** — re-engage churned customers

### Compounding Reinvestment Loop
```
Acquire customer → Recover CAC in 30 days → Reinvest in highest-ROI Core Four channel
→ Acquire more customers → Recover CAC in 30 days → Reinvest...
```
The business becomes a perpetual motion machine funded by its own economics.

## Simple Money Model Diagnostic

Answer these questions for your business:

1. **What is your CAC?** (Total marketing + sales cost ÷ new customers)
2. **What revenue does a new customer generate in the first 30 days?**
3. **Is #2 > #1?** → If yes, you can scale. If no, money model is broken.
4. **What is your average LTV?** (Average revenue over average customer lifetime)
5. **LTV : CAC ratio** → Healthy is 3:1 or better. Under 2:1, the model is fragile.
