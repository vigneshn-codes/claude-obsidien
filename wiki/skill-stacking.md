# Skill Stacking

**Summary:** The mental model that entrepreneurship is primarily about acquiring skills, beliefs, and character traits — identifying and closing gaps at each stage is the highest-leverage activity.

**Category:** Mindset & Identity
**Sources:** `05-key-frameworks.md`, `06-quotes.md`
**Related:** [[mindset-identity]], [[divergent-thinking]]

---

## The Core Idea

> "Entrepreneurship is about acquiring skills, beliefs, and character traits. To advance, determine which you lack."

At every stage of business (and life), growth stalls because of a missing skill, a limiting belief, or an underdeveloped character trait. The gap is always identifiable if you're honest about where exactly things break down.

---

## The Framework

1. **Diagnose the current ceiling** — Where specifically does performance plateau?
2. **Attribute it honestly** — Is it a skill gap? A belief gap? A character/habit gap?
3. **Target the specific gap** — Acquire the missing element through deliberate study, practice, or exposure.
4. **Outputs follow inputs** — Revenue, growth, and results are lagging indicators of skills/beliefs/traits acquired earlier.

> "Your income is a lagging indicator of the skills you've acquired."

---

## The Three Gap Types

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Skill gap** | You don't know how to do something | Can't write a cold email that converts |
| **Belief gap** | You believe something limiting that isn't true | "I can't charge premium prices in my market" |
| **Character/habit gap** | You know what to do but don't do it consistently | Know the [[rule-of-100]] but don't follow through daily |

Misdiagnosing the type leads to the wrong fix. Trying to train your way out of a belief gap doesn't work; reading your way out of a character gap doesn't work.

---

## Acquisition Hierarchy

> "Read a book. Watch a video. Take a course. Then ship something. Repeat for a decade."

Learning without shipping produces knowledge but not skill. Shipping without learning produces iteration without improvement. The cycle is: learn → apply → gather feedback → learn again.

---

## Application to Software-Coding / Workout Programming

For a technical founder building a workout product, the relevant skill stack includes:

| Domain | Skills to Build | Belief to Acquire | Habit to Develop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | UX, onboarding design, retention mechanics | "Simplicity beats features" | Weekly user interview |
| Marketing | [[core-four]], [[lead-magnet]] design, content | "I can market without being salesy" | Daily content or outreach |
| Offer | [[value-equation]], [[grand-slam-offer]] construction | "I can charge premium" | Quarterly offer audit |
| Money | [[cac-payback]] math, LTV modeling | "Unit economics must work at scale" | Monthly P&L review |

---

## The Leverage Frame

> "Skill acquisition is the highest-leverage activity."

Acquiring a skill has compounding returns — you use it for decades. Hiring for a skill you don't have has diminishing returns — you can't manage or improve what you can't evaluate. Build skills first; hire second.
