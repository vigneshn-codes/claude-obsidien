# Value Equation

**Summary:** Hormozi's mathematical model for perceived value — four variables that, when manipulated, control how compelling any offer feels to a prospect.

**Category:** Offer Design
**Sources:** `07-concept-value-equation.md`, `01-100m-offers.md`, `05-key-frameworks.md`
**Related:** [[grand-slam-offer]], [[trim-and-stack]], [[guarantee-taxonomy]], [[bonuses-stack]]

## The Formula

```
            Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                 Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Because the formula uses division, **as the denominator approaches zero, value approaches infinity.** Reducing friction is at least as powerful as amplifying outcome.

---

## The Four Variables

### Numerator — Increase These

**Dream Outcome**
The felt transformation the prospect imagines after success. Sell the *status*, the *relief from pain*, the *identity shift* — not the deliverable. "Lose 20 lbs" is a deliverable; "Feel confident in shorts again at your high school reunion" is a Dream Outcome.

**Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**
How confident the prospect is that *this specific offer* will deliver. Driven by:
- Case studies and testimonials
- Named mechanisms ("our proprietary 4-week progressive overload protocol")
- Credentials and track record
- Guarantees (see [[guarantee-taxonomy]])

### Denominator — Decrease These

**Time Delay**
The gap between payment and perceived benefit. Shorter always wins. Tactics:
- Quick wins in the first 24–72 hours
- Instant access to materials
- Done-for-you components
- Clear "you'll feel X by day 7" milestones

**Effort & Sacrifice**
Ancillary work, required decisions, learning curve, lifestyle disruption. Tactics:
- Templates and automation
- Removed prerequisites
- Simplified UX
- Done-with-you or done-for-you delivery

## The Perception Insight

> The London Underground installed arrival-time displays. Rider satisfaction increased more from *knowing the wait* than from actually shortening it.

**Perception matters more than reality.** A workout program that *feels* fast and achievable converts better than an objectively superior one that *feels* long and hard — even if outcomes are identical. Design the perception first, then back-fill the substance.

---

## How to Score and Improve Your Offer

1. Score your current offer 1–10 on each variable.
2. Identify the lowest-scoring variable.
3. Brainstorm 10 ways to improve *only that variable*.
4. Ship the change. Re-score.

| Variable | Weak Score Looks Like | Strong Score Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | "Get fit" | "Pack on 15 lbs of muscle before your summer trip" |
| Perceived Likelihood | No proof, vague process | 47 before/afters, named 12-week protocol, money-back |
| Time Delay | "Results in 6 months" | "Feel stronger in week 1. See visible change by week 4." |
| Effort & Sacrifice | "Requires 2-hour workouts 6x/week" | "30 min, 4x/week, no equipment needed" |

---

## Application to Workout Programming / Fitness Software

| Context | Value Equation Lever |
|---|---|
| App onboarding | Decrease Time Delay — show a win on day 1 (first workout logged, first PR set) |
| Subscription pricing | Increase Perceived Likelihood — show aggregate user stats ("avg user adds 40 lbs to their squat in 8 weeks") |
| Feature design | Decrease Effort — auto-generate next workout, no planning required |
| Landing page copy | Increase Dream Outcome — lead with the transformation, not the feature list |
